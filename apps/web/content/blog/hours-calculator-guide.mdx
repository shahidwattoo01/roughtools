To add hours and minutes: sum the minutes separately, convert every 60 minutes to 1 hour, then add the resulting hours. 3:45 + 2:30 = 5 hours 75 minutes = 6 hours 15 minutes. To convert decimal hours to hours and minutes: the whole number is hours; multiply the decimal by 60 for minutes. 7.75 hours = 7 hours 45 minutes. 2.333 hours = 2 hours 20 minutes.

Time calculations are tricky because time uses base-60 (not base-10) — you cannot simply add 3.45 + 2.30 in decimal and expect the right answer. A timesheet showing 3 hours 45 minutes must be converted to 3.75 hours (or handled as 3h45m) for arithmetic operations.

## Adding Hours and Minutes

**Method 1: Add hours and minutes separately**
```
  8 h 45 min
+ 6 h 50 min
-----------
 14 h 95 min = 14 h + 1 h 35 min = 15 h 35 min
```

**Method 2: Convert everything to minutes first**
8h 45m = 525 min
6h 50m = 410 min
Total: 935 min = 935/60 = 15h 35min

## Decimal Hours vs. Hours:Minutes

Payroll, billing, and project management systems often use **decimal hours** (3.5 hours) instead of hours:minutes (3:30). Conversion:

```
Hours:Minutes → Decimal: Decimal = Hours + (Minutes / 60)
Decimal → Hours:Minutes: Minutes = Decimal fractional part × 60
```

**Quick reference:**

| Hours:Min | Decimal | Hours:Min | Decimal |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| 0:15 | 0.25 | 4:00 | 4.00 |
| 0:30 | 0.50 | 4:30 | 4.50 |
| 0:45 | 0.75 | 5:00 | 5.00 |
| 1:00 | 1.00 | 7:30 | 7.50 |
| 1:30 | 1.50 | 7:45 | 7.75 |
| 2:00 | 2.00 | 8:00 | 8.00 |

## Calculating Pay from Hours

```
Regular pay = Hours worked × Hourly rate
Overtime = Max(0, Hours − 40) × Hourly rate × 1.5
Total pay = Regular hours pay + Overtime pay
```

For 42.75 hours at $20/hour:
- Regular: 40 hours × $20 = $800
- Overtime: 2.75 hours × $30 = $82.50
- **Total: $882.50**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why can't I just add 3:45 + 2:30 = 6:15 in Excel?**
Spreadsheet programs store time as a fraction of 24 hours — 1 hour = 1/24 = 0.04167. When you add time cells, you're adding these fractions, which works correctly for times within a day. For elapsed hours totaling over 24, format the result cell as [h]:mm (with brackets around h) to show total hours rather than wrapping around midnight. Otherwise 25 hours would display as 1:00 (1 AM the next day).

**How do I calculate hours worked from clock-in/clock-out times?**
Clock-out − Clock-in = total duration. Subtract any unpaid break time. For example: Clock in 8:00 AM, Clock out 5:30 PM = 9.5 hours total. Minus 30-minute lunch = 9.0 hours worked = 9:00. Be consistent about whether break time is paid or unpaid — the FLSA requires tracking compensable time but does not dictate how breaks are recorded.

**How many hours is a part-time job vs. full-time?**
Under the Affordable Care Act, full-time employment is defined as 30+ hours per week for benefits eligibility purposes. For traditional purposes, full-time is typically 35–40 hours/week. Part-time is anything under full-time, commonly 15–30 hours/week. Overtime pay under the FLSA kicks in at 40+ hours/week (not 35+) for non-exempt employees.

**How do I convert between time zones for scheduling?**
Time zone conversion is not just addition/subtraction — it also involves Daylight Saving Time (DST) transitions. For example, New York is UTC−5 (EST) in winter and UTC−4 (EDT) in summer. London is UTC+0 (GMT) in winter and UTC+1 (BST) in summer. The offset between them changes at DST transitions. For reliable conversion, use a time zone converter tool that accounts for DST.

**How many hours are in a year?**
365 days × 24 hours = 8,760 hours/year. In a leap year: 366 × 24 = 8,784 hours. A standard work year (40 hours/week × 52 weeks) = 2,080 hours. Accounting for 10 paid holidays: 2,080 − (10 × 8) = 2,000 billable hours. This is why consultants often quote rates with "up to 2,000 billable hours/year" as the practical maximum.
