## Why Weighing Is More Accurate Than Volume

Volume measurements of dry ingredients can vary by 20–30% depending on how the ingredient is measured. A cup of flour can weigh anywhere from 100g to 160g depending on whether it's sifted, spooned loosely, or packed:

- **Sifted flour (lightest):** ~95–100g per cup
- **Spooned into cup:** ~120g per cup (standard)
- **Scooped and leveled:** ~140–150g per cup
- **Packed:** ~165g per cup

Professional bakers and most international recipes use weight (grams) rather than volume precisely because of this variation. If your bread isn't rising correctly or your cookies are spreading too much, incorrect flour measurement is often the cause.

## Scaling Recipes

To scale a recipe up or down, multiply every ingredient by the scaling factor:
```
Scaling Factor = Desired Servings / Original Servings

Scaled Amount = Original Amount × Scaling Factor
```

**Scaling a 12-serving cookie recipe down to 4 servings:**
- Scaling factor = 4/12 = 0.333
- 3 cups flour × 0.333 = 1 cup flour
- 2 eggs × 0.333 = 0.667 eggs (use 2/3 egg — beat 1 egg, use 2/3 of it, or substitute with 1 yolk)
- 1 tsp baking soda × 0.333 = 1/3 tsp

**Scaling challenges:**
- Eggs don't divide easily — see egg substitution methods
- Leavening (baking powder, baking soda) doesn't scale linearly for very large batches
- Pan size changes affect baking time and temperature
- Some spices and seasonings shouldn't scale proportionally in large batches

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many teaspoons are in a tablespoon?**
3 teaspoons = 1 tablespoon (US). This is one of the most commonly needed conversions: a recipe calling for 3 tsp of vanilla can be measured as 1 tbsp. A recipe calling for 1/2 tablespoon = 1.5 teaspoons. Important: UK tablespoons are slightly different (15 ml standard, but some sources cite UK tablespoon as 14.2 ml or Australian tablespoon as 20 ml). US and UK teaspoons are both 5 ml — identical.

**Is 1 fluid ounce the same as 1 ounce by weight?**
No — these are completely different units. A fluid ounce (fl oz) is a volume measure: 1 fl oz = 30 ml = 2 tablespoons. An ounce (oz) is a weight/mass measure: 1 oz = 28.35 grams. Water happens to be close (1 fl oz of water ≈ 1.04 oz by weight), but most other substances differ significantly. 1 fl oz of honey weighs approximately 1.5 oz by weight (honey is denser than water). Always check whether a recipe means fl oz or oz by weight.

**What does "metric cup" mean?**
In Australia, New Zealand, and Canada, the metric cup = 250 ml (versus 236.6 ml for US cup). The difference (13.4 ml) is small for most recipes but noticeable in precision baking. Australian tablespoon = 20 ml (vs. 15 ml US). UK tablespoon = 15 ml (same as US). When using international recipes, check the source country's measurement standards. Most modern Australian and Canadian recipes use metric grams and milliliters rather than cups.

**How do I convert a recipe from grams to cups?**
Divide the grams by the weight per cup for that specific ingredient. 300g flour: 300 ÷ 120 = 2.5 cups. 150g butter: 150 ÷ 227 = 0.66 cups ≈ 2/3 cup. The free RoughTools cooking converter includes gram-to-cup conversion for 50+ common baking ingredients with ingredient-specific density values.

**What is the difference between dry measuring cups and liquid measuring cups?**
Dry measuring cups are designed to be filled to the brim and leveled off. They come in exact sizes (1/4 cup, 1/3 cup, 1/2 cup, 1 cup). Liquid measuring cups have graduated markings, a pour spout, and are read at eye level with the cup on a flat surface. Using a liquid measuring cup for dry ingredients (or vice versa) introduces inaccuracy. Dry measuring cups used for liquids won't fill to the exact measurement line because you can't level a liquid.
