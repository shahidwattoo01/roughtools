## What Is an Hourly to Salary Calculator?

An hourly to salary calculator converts a wage expressed as dollars per hour into its equivalent annual, monthly, bi-weekly, and weekly amounts — and can also reverse the calculation, converting a stated annual salary back to an hourly rate.

The core formula is straightforward: Annual Salary = Hourly Rate × Hours per Week × Weeks per Year. The standard full-time assumption is 40 hours per week and 52 weeks, producing a multiplier of 2,080. Variations for part-time workers, contractors, or those with unpaid leave use different hour and week inputs.

This calculation matters in several real-world situations. When comparing a salaried job offer to an hourly position, converting both to annual equivalents puts them on the same basis. When negotiating pay, knowing your effective hourly rate helps you evaluate whether a salary increase is meaningful. When budgeting monthly expenses against a bi-weekly or weekly paycheck, the conversion to monthly income is essential.

The calculator also handles overtime, which is paid at 1.5× the regular rate for hours beyond 40 per week under US federal law. Workers with regular overtime can use it to see how those extra hours affect annual take-home — often significantly more than the base annual salary implies.

## The Hourly to Annual Salary Formula

```
Annual Salary = Hourly Rate × Hours per Week × Weeks per Year

Standard full-time: Hourly × 40 × 52 = Hourly × 2,080
```

**Derivations:**
- Monthly = Annual ÷ 12
- Semi-monthly (twice/month) = Annual ÷ 24
- Bi-weekly (every 2 weeks) = Annual ÷ 26
- Weekly = Annual ÷ 52
- Daily (8-hour day) = Hourly × 8

## Overtime Calculation

For hourly employees, overtime (over 40 hours/week under FLSA) is paid at 1.5× the regular rate:

```
Overtime Rate = Regular Rate × 1.5
Weekly Pay with Overtime = (40 × Reg. Rate) + (OT Hours × OT Rate)
```

At $20/hour working 45 hours:
- Regular: 40 × $20 = $800
- Overtime: 5 × ($20 × 1.5) = 5 × $30 = $150
- **Total: $950**

| Benefit | Annual Value |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does $15 minimum wage equal a living wage?**
At the federal $15 minimum wage: $15 × 2,080 = $31,200/year. The MIT Living Wage Calculator estimates that a living wage for a single adult (no children) in most US cities is $24,000–$40,000/year, depending on location. In high cost-of-living cities (San Francisco, New York), a living wage for a single adult can exceed $50,000/year. So $15/hour reaches a living wage in many low-cost areas but falls short in major metros.

**How do I calculate my take-home pay from hourly wage?**
Gross annual salary = Hourly × 2,080. Net take-home depends on tax filing status, deductions, and state. For a rough estimate, use 75–80% of gross as take-home in most US states (accounting for ~22–25% combined federal income tax + FICA for middle incomes). A $20/hour worker ($41,600 gross) takes home approximately $31,200–$34,500/year after taxes.

**What is bi-weekly vs. semi-monthly pay?**
Bi-weekly = paid every 2 weeks = 26 pay periods per year. Semi-monthly = paid twice per month = 24 pay periods per year. For the same annual salary, bi-weekly paychecks are slightly smaller (÷26) than semi-monthly (÷24). Bi-weekly workers get two "extra" paychecks per year — two months they receive 3 paychecks instead of 2. This is not extra income, just a timing difference.

**Should I negotiate hourly or salary?**
If you expect to work overtime regularly, an hourly arrangement is generally better because overtime (1.5× pay) adds up quickly. Salaried employees (in exempt positions) typically don't receive overtime regardless of hours worked. However, salary generally comes with more stability, benefits, and perceived professional status. If offered a salaried position at the equivalent rate, negotiate for the higher end if you anticipate long hours.

**What hours should I use if I don't work exactly 40 hours?**
Use your actual average weekly hours. Part-time at 30 hours: Annual = Hourly × 30 × 52 = Hourly × 1,560. 35 hours: Annual = Hourly × 1,820. The calculator on RoughTools lets you specify custom hours/week and weeks/year (accounting for unpaid time off).
