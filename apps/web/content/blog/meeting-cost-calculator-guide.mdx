To calculate the cost of a meeting, multiply the hourly fully-loaded labor cost of each attendee by the meeting duration, then sum across all attendees. A 1-hour meeting with 8 employees averaging $50/hour (fully-loaded): 8 × $50 = $400 per hour, times 1 hour = **$400 cost**. A 2-hour leadership meeting with 5 people at $100/hour = **$1,000**.

Organizations lose enormous amounts of money to unproductive meetings. Microsoft research found that the average employee spends 252 hours per year in meetings, and 67% of meetings are considered failures by at least one attendee. A company of 100 people with an average salary of $60,000 spending 30% of time in meetings spends approximately $1.8 million per year on meeting time — without calculating opportunity cost of work not done.

## Meeting Cost Formula

```
Hourly Cost per Person = Annual Salary × Overhead Multiplier / 2,080 hours

Meeting Total Cost = Sum of (Hourly Cost per Person × Meeting Hours) for all attendees
```

## The Hidden Costs Beyond the Room

**Opportunity cost:** Every person in the meeting is not doing their other work. A developer in a 1-hour meeting could have written ~100 lines of code; a sales rep could have made 10 calls.

**Preparation and follow-up time:** For every 1 hour of meeting, expect 30–60 minutes of prep and 30 minutes of follow-up actions per attendee. A 1-hour meeting for 8 people may consume 16–20 total hours of actual effort.

**Context switching cost:** Research suggests that interrupting deep work (flow state) takes 23 minutes to recover. Meetings in the middle of the day fragment otherwise productive blocks.

## Signs a Meeting is Costing More Than It Delivers

- No clear agenda distributed in advance
- No decision is needed — information could be shared via email or Slack
- Over 6 attendees for a decision-making meeting
- Attendees arrive late or leave early without noticing the impact
- Action items from the previous meeting are not reviewed
- Meeting regularly runs over its scheduled time
- Attendees are looking at phones or doing other work during the meeting

## How to Make Meetings Worth Their Cost

**Amazon's "6-pager" model:** Start meetings with 30 minutes of silent reading of a detailed written memo. This ensures everyone is informed before discussion begins, dramatically improving meeting quality.

**The "two pizza rule" (Jeff Bezos):** If you can't feed the meeting attendees with two pizzas, the meeting is too large. ~6–8 people is the maximum for effective decision-making. For larger groups, consider a "informed/involved" split: decision-makers in the room, others receive meeting notes.

**Meeting audit:** For one week, track all meetings you attend. Calculate the hourly cost. Ask for each: was this meeting necessary? Could this have been an email? Could 3 people make this decision instead of 10? Most organizations find 20–30% of meetings could be eliminated with no impact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a "fully-loaded" labor cost?**
Fully-loaded (or burdened) labor cost includes: base salary/wages, employer-paid taxes (FICA: 7.65%), health insurance, life insurance, 401k matching, PTO (paid time off is a labor cost even when not "working"), training, and a share of overhead costs (office space, HR, IT). For US salaried employees, the fully-loaded cost is typically 1.25–1.5× base salary. A $80,000/year employee has a fully-loaded cost of $100,000–$120,000/year.

**How do I reduce meeting culture at my company?**
Culture changes require leadership modeling. Effective approaches: declare "meeting-free" blocks (e.g., mornings for deep work); require agendas and send cancellation for meetings without them; track meeting time like any other resource (meetings are not free); shift status updates to async tools (Slack, project management software); limit standing recurrings to 30 minutes and require justification to renew them quarterly.

**What's the cost of a 30-minute meeting that could have been an email?**
At $70,000 average salary with 1.4× overhead, hourly cost per person = $47.12. Six people in a 30-minute "could have been an email" meeting: 6 × $23.56 = $141. If this happens 3 times per week for 50 weeks: $141 × 150 = **$21,150/year** in wasted labor costs, not counting opportunity cost and context switching.

**How many hours should I spend in meetings per week?**
Industry research suggests knowledge workers are most productive when meetings consume 15–25% of their work week (6–10 hours out of 40). Managers typically need 30–40% meeting time; individual contributors ideally 10–20%. When meetings exceed 35–40% of a knowledge worker's week, productivity and job satisfaction both suffer. Startups often find employees at 50–60% meeting time during rapid growth phases, which is unsustainable.

**How should remote meetings be counted in this calculation?**
Exactly the same. Remote meetings (video calls) carry the same attendee cost as in-person meetings — the attendees are still spending time in the meeting instead of doing other work. Some argue remote meetings have slightly lower costs (no physical travel) and slightly higher hidden costs (video fatigue, technical friction). The base cost formula is identical.
