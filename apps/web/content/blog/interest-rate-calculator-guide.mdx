## What Is an Interest Rate Calculator?

An interest rate calculator computes how much interest accrues on a principal amount over time, based on the interest rate, compounding frequency, and term. It works for both savings (projecting how an investment grows) and borrowing (calculating the total cost of a loan).

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal: I = P × r × t. It is used for short-term loans and some bonds. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus all previously earned interest — interest on interest — which causes balances to grow exponentially. Most savings accounts, CDs, mortgages, and credit cards use compound interest.

Compounding frequency matters. The same 5% annual rate compounded monthly produces more interest than compounded annually, because interest is calculated more often and added to the balance sooner. The formula: A = P × (1 + r/n)^(nt), where n is the number of compounding periods per year.

APR (Annual Percentage Rate) and APY (Annual Percentage Yield) describe the same rate from different angles. APR is the nominal rate stated by lenders — it ignores compounding effects within the year. APY is the effective annual rate including compounding, showing actual growth or cost. For comparing savings accounts, use APY. For comparing loans, use APR. Converting between them: APY = (1 + APR/n)^n − 1.

## Simple vs. Compound Interest Formulas

**Simple Interest:**
```
I = P × r × t
A = P + I = P(1 + rt)

Where: P = Principal, r = Annual rate (decimal), t = Time (years)
```

**Compound Interest:**
```
A = P × (1 + r/n)^(nt)
I = A - P

Where: n = Compounding periods per year
```

Compounding frequencies:
- Annual: n = 1
- Semi-annual: n = 2
- Quarterly: n = 4
- Monthly: n = 12
- Daily: n = 365

**Continuous compounding:**
```
A = P × e^(rt)
```

## APR vs. APY: The Critical Difference

**APR (Annual Percentage Rate):** The nominal interest rate, ignoring compounding effects within the year. Used for loans (mortgages, credit cards, car loans) to show the annual rate you're charged.

**APY (Annual Percentage Yield):** The effective annual rate that includes the effect of compounding within the year. Used for savings accounts and CDs to show actual growth.

```
APY = (1 + APR/n)^n - 1
```

A savings account with 5.00% APR compounded monthly: APY = (1 + 0.05/12)¹² − 1 = (1.004167)¹² − 1 = **5.116% APY**.

A credit card with 24% APR compounded daily: APY = (1 + 0.24/365)³⁶⁵ − 1 = **27.11% APY** (the real cost).

**Rule:** For savings, higher APY = more growth. For loans, higher APR = higher cost. Always compare the same measure (APY to APY) when evaluating similar products.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Rule of 72?**
The Rule of 72 estimates how long it takes to double money at compound interest: Years to Double = 72 ÷ Annual Interest Rate. At 6%: 72/6 = 12 years to double. At 8%: 9 years. At 12%: 6 years. This approximation works best for rates between 5–15%. For more precision, use the exact formula: t = ln(2) / ln(1 + r) = 0.693 / ln(1.08) = 0.693 / 0.0770 = 9.0 years at 8%.

**What is the difference between nominal and real interest rate?**
Nominal interest rate is the stated rate. Real interest rate adjusts for inflation: Real Rate ≈ Nominal Rate − Inflation Rate (Fisher equation: exact formula is (1 + nominal) / (1 + inflation) − 1). If your savings account earns 4.5% APY and inflation is 3.2%, your real return is approximately 1.3%. This is the actual increase in purchasing power. During periods of high inflation, even relatively good nominal interest rates may produce negative real returns (your money buys less despite growing numerically).

**How does credit card interest actually get calculated?**
Credit card companies typically charge interest daily on the outstanding balance: Daily Periodic Rate = APR / 365. Monthly interest = Average Daily Balance × DPR × Days in Month. A $3,000 average balance at 24% APR: DPR = 24%/365 = 0.0658%. Monthly interest = $3,000 × 0.000658 × 30 = **$59.20**. The insidious part: unpaid interest is added to the principal, and next month's interest is calculated on the higher balance (interest on interest). This is why minimum payment strategies take so long — most of the payment goes to interest, barely reducing principal.

**What is negative interest?**
Negative interest rates occur when central banks set benchmark rates below zero, meaning banks pay to hold money (or more precisely, central banks charge banks for depositing reserves). Some European countries (Switzerland, Japan) implemented negative rates to stimulate lending. Retail depositors rarely see negative rates — banks typically lower rates to 0% rather than charging customers. Negative real interest rates (nominal rate below inflation) are more common and effectively erode the real value of savings.

**How do I calculate the interest on a savings account monthly statement?**
Find the monthly statement showing: opening balance, APY (or APR), and interest earned. Verify: Opening balance × (APY/12) ≈ interest earned for the month (approximate, since APY is annualized). If opening balance = $8,000 at 4.5% APY: monthly interest ≈ $8,000 × 0.045/12 = $30. Small differences from actual paid interest arise from daily compounding (the actual method) vs. this monthly approximation.
