## What Is Volume?

Volume is the amount of three-dimensional space enclosed within a solid shape. It is measured in cubic units — cubic inches, cubic feet, cubic centimeters, cubic meters — or in liquid equivalents like liters and gallons. Every solid shape has its own volume formula derived from its geometry.

The simplest shape is the rectangular prism (box): multiply length times width times height. A box that is 2 feet by 3 feet by 4 feet contains 24 cubic feet of space. More complex shapes like spheres and cones require formulas involving pi (π) and radius or height. A cylinder's volume is the area of its circular base (π × r²) multiplied by its height.

Volume matters across a wide range of practical applications. In construction, you need to know the volume of a concrete slab, a trench, or a fill area to order the right amount of material — typically sold by the cubic yard. In pool maintenance, total gallons determine how much chemical to add. In aquariums, gallons determine stocking capacity and filtration requirements. In shipping, dimensional weight pricing depends on box volume.

The challenge is knowing which formula applies to which shape, and keeping units consistent throughout the calculation. ## Volume Formulas for Common 3D Shapes

| Shape | Formula | Variables |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| Rectangular Prism (Box) | V = l × w × h | l = length, w = width, h = height |
| Cube | V = s³ | s = side length |
| Cylinder | V = π × r² × h | r = radius, h = height |
| Sphere | V = (4/3) × π × r³ | r = radius |
| Cone | V = (1/3) × π × r² × h | r = base radius, h = height |
| Pyramid | V = (1/3) × base area × h | h = perpendicular height |
| Triangular Prism | V = (1/2 × b × h_t) × l | b = base, h_t = triangle height, l = prism length |
| Ellipsoid | V = (4/3) × π × a × b × c | a, b, c = semi-axes |

**Worked examples:**

**Concrete slab:**
Dimensions: 20 ft × 10 ft × 4 inches (= 0.333 ft)
Volume = 20 × 10 × 0.333 = 66.7 cubic feet
Convert: 66.7 ÷ 27 = 2.47 cubic yards of concrete (concrete is sold by the cubic yard)

**Swimming pool:**
Rectangular: 30 ft × 15 ft × 5 ft average depth
Volume = 30 × 15 × 5 = 2,250 cubic feet
Convert: 2,250 × 7.481 = 16,832 gallons

**Water tank (cylindrical):**
Radius = 3 ft, Height = 8 ft
Volume = π × 3² × 8 = 3.14159 × 9 × 8 = 226.2 cubic feet = 1,691 gallons

**Sphere — basketball:**
Diameter = 9.4 inches → Radius = 4.7 inches
Volume = (4/3) × π × 4.7³ = 4.189 × 103.8 = 434.9 cubic inches

## Practical Volume Applications

**Mulch/topsoil calculation:**
Volume needed (cubic feet) = Area (sq ft) × Depth (ft)
A 200 sq ft garden bed with 3" of mulch: 200 × 0.25 = 50 cubic feet
Convert to cubic yards: 50 ÷ 27 = 1.85 cubic yards (mulch is sold by the cubic yard)

**Aquarium capacity:**
Convert cubic inches to gallons: Volume (in³) ÷ 231 = US gallons
A 24"×12"×16" tank: (24×12×16) ÷ 231 = 4,608 ÷ 231 = 19.9 gallons

**Fuel tank:**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many cubic feet are in a cubic yard?**
One cubic yard = 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft = 27 cubic feet. This conversion is essential for materials sold by the cubic yard: concrete, mulch, topsoil, gravel, fill dirt. If you need 270 cubic feet of fill dirt, you need 270 ÷ 27 = 10 cubic yards.

**What is the volume of a gallon in cubic inches?**
One US gallon = 231 cubic inches exactly (by legal definition). One UK gallon = 277.4 cubic inches. This is why swimming pool calculations and aquarium sizing often convert from cubic inches to gallons by dividing by 231.

**How do I calculate the volume of an irregular 3D shape?**
Methods: (1) Water displacement — submerge the object in a known volume of water; the volume increase equals the object's volume. (2) Divide into regular shapes — decompose the irregular shape into cubes, cylinders, and pyramids, calculate each, and sum. (3) 3D scanning with software can calculate precise volumes of complex shapes.

**How much concrete is needed for a foundation?**
Calculate the volume of each foundation section (walls, footings, slabs) in cubic feet using the rectangular prism formula. Sum all sections. Divide by 27 to get cubic yards (standard concrete ordering unit). Add 10% waste/overage. Concrete suppliers deliver by the cubic yard; minimum orders are often 1 cubic yard ($150–$250 depending on location).

**What is bulk density and why does it matter for weight?**
Volume tells you the space something takes up; bulk density tells you how heavy it is per unit volume. Concrete: ~150 lbs/cubic foot. Water: 62.4 lbs/cubic foot (= 8.34 lbs/gallon). Soil: 80–100 lbs/cubic foot. Gravel: 100–110 lbs/cubic foot. To find weight: Weight = Volume × Bulk Density. A 10 cubic yard concrete pour weighs: 10 × 27 × 150 = 40,500 lbs (20.25 tons).
