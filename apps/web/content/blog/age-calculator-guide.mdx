To calculate age from a birthdate, subtract the birth year from the current year, then adjust if the birthday hasn't occurred yet this year. Born October 15, 1992, and today is June 14, 2026: 2026 − 1992 = 34, but October hasn't come yet in 2026, so age = **33 years**. For more precision, also calculate the months (8 months since last birthday) and days (from Oct 15 to Jun 14 = ~241 days).

## How the Age Calculation Works

The formula: Age = Current Date − Birth Date

For "how old am I" calculations, compare the month and day to determine if the birthday has passed this year:

```
If today's month/day >= birthday month/day:
    Age = Current Year − Birth Year

If today's month/day < birthday month/day:
    Age = Current Year − Birth Year − 1
```

## Age in Different Time Units

| Unit | Formula | Example (age 30) |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| Years | Direct calculation | 30 |
| Months | Years × 12 + extra months | ~360 |
| Weeks | Days / 7 | ~1,565 |
| Days | Years × 365.25 | ~10,957 |
| Hours | Days × 24 | ~262,974 |
| Minutes | Hours × 60 | ~15,778,440 |
| Seconds | Minutes × 60 | ~946,706,400 |

The 365.25 accounts for leap years (one extra day every 4 years on average).

## What Day of the Week Were You Born?

The age calculator can determine your birth day of the week using the Doomsday algorithm or calendar arithmetic. Interestingly, you cannot be born on a different day of the week than your birthday every year — your birthday shifts one day per year (two days in leap years). If born on a Tuesday in 2000, your 26th birthday in a non-leap year following the pattern would be: Tuesday + 26 days shift (adjusted for leap years) = specific day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How does the calculator handle February 29 (leap year) birthdays?**
People born on February 29 technically have a birthday only every 4 years. For age calculation purposes, most conventions treat February 28 or March 1 of non-leap years as their "official" birthday. The age is the same either way; the day of celebration differs by convention. Some jurisdictions have specific legal rules for leap day birthdays — in the UK, the legal birthday is March 1 in non-leap years.

**What is the youngest possible age to vote in any US state?**
18 in all US states (since the 26th Amendment in 1971). Some states allow 17-year-olds to vote in primary elections if they will be 18 by the general election, but the voting age for general elections is universally 18. Globally, some countries allow voting at 16 (Austria, Scotland for local elections, some Latin American countries).

**How do I calculate someone's age from a historical date?**
The formula is the same — subtract birth year from the target date year, adjusting for whether the birthday has passed in that year. For historical age calculations, be aware of calendar changes: the Gregorian calendar was adopted in England in 1752 (and different countries adopted it at different times). Dates before your country's Gregorian adoption may be Julian calendar dates, which differ by up to 13 days.

**What is the "age of criminal responsibility" in different countries?**
The age of criminal responsibility varies widely: 10 in England, Wales, and Australia; 12 in Canada, Netherlands; 14 in Germany, Italy, Japan; 15 in Scandinavia; 18 in Belgium and Luxembourg. In the US, states set their own rules — typically no specific minimum age at the federal level, though most states use 6–10 years as a floor.

**How do East Asian age systems differ from Western age?**
Traditional Korean (and formerly used Japanese and Chinese) age systems count age differently: a person is considered 1 at birth (not 0), and everyone ages on January 1st, not their birthday. So a baby born in December is 2 years old by Korean traditional reckoning by January of the following year. South Korea officially abolished this system in 2023, adopting the international standard, though traditional age counting persists socially.
