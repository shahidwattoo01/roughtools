Roman numerals use letter symbols (I, V, X, L, C, D, M) to represent numbers through additive and subtractive notation. To convert a Roman numeral to decimal, add the values left to right, subtracting when a smaller value precedes a larger one. XLVII = XL (40) + V (5) + II (2) = 47. To convert decimal to Roman numeral, repeatedly subtract the largest symbol value that fits.

Roman numerals appear on clock faces, Super Bowl numbers, movie copyright dates, monarchs (King Henry VIII), Olympiads, book chapters, and building cornerstones. MMXXVI is 2026 — the current year in Roman numerals.

## The Subtractive Notation Rules

When a smaller value appears before a larger value, subtract the smaller from the larger:
- IV = 5 − 1 = **4** (not IIII)
- IX = 10 − 1 = **9** (not VIIII)
- XL = 50 − 10 = **40** (not XXXX)
- XC = 100 − 10 = **90** (not LXXXX)
- CD = 500 − 100 = **400** (not CCCC)
- CM = 1,000 − 100 = **900** (not DCCCC)

**Rules for subtractive notation:**
- Only I, X, and C can be used subtractively
- A numeral can only be subtracted from the next two values in the sequence
  - I can precede V (IV=4) and X (IX=9)
  - X can precede L (XL=40) and C (XC=90)
  - C can precede D (CD=400) and M (CM=900)

## Decimal to Roman Numeral Conversion

To convert 1,994 to Roman numerals:
1. 1,000 = M → remainder 994
2. 900 = CM → remainder 94
3. 90 = XC → remainder 4
4. 4 = IV → remainder 0
5. **1,994 = MCMXCIV**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why isn't there a Roman numeral for zero?**
Roman numerals developed from tally marks used in trade and were additive — you counted up from amounts. The concept of zero as a number (not just the absence of quantity) was developed by Indian mathematicians and introduced to Europe via Arab scholars in the Middle Ages. The lack of zero was a significant limitation of Roman arithmetic — it made complex calculations extremely difficult compared to positional decimal or binary systems.

**What is the largest number in Roman numerals?**
Traditional Roman numerals can represent up to 3,999 (MMMCMXCIX). The symbol for 4,000 would require MMMM (four Ms), which violates the "maximum three consecutive identical symbols" rule. For larger numbers, a vinculum (horizontal bar over a symbol) multiplies its value by 1,000 — so M̄ = 1,000,000 — but this convention is rarely used today. Most modern implementations cap at 3,999.

**Can you perform arithmetic with Roman numerals?**
Basic addition and subtraction are possible but cumbersome. Multiplication and division are extremely difficult in Roman numerals. This is why Roman civilization relied on the abacus for calculations and used Roman numerals primarily for record-keeping. The adoption of Hindu-Arabic numerals (0-9) with positional notation transformed mathematics and commerce by making written arithmetic practical.

**What year is MMXXVI?**
MMXXVI = MM (2,000) + XX (20) + VI (6) = **2026**. When you see a copyright date or movie release on screen in Roman numerals, read each group left to right: MM = 2,000; XX = 20; VI = 6; total = 2,026.

**Why does the Super Bowl use Roman numerals?**
The NFL adopted Roman numerals for Super Bowls to avoid confusion between the game number and the year it's played. Super Bowl 50 (played in 2016) was an exception — they used Arabic numerals because "Super Bowl L" was considered awkward and hard to read visually. Super Bowl 51 (2017) returned to Roman numerals as LI.
