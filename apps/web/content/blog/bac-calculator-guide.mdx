Blood Alcohol Content (BAC) is the percentage of alcohol in your bloodstream by weight. The Widmark formula: BAC = (Alcohol consumed in grams / (Body weight in grams × r)) − (0.015 × Hours), where r = body water constant (0.68 for males, 0.55 for females). For a 150 lb (68 kg) male drinking 3 beers (14g alcohol each) in 2 hours: BAC = (42 / (68,000 × 0.68)) − (0.015 × 2) = 0.091% − 0.030% = **0.061% BAC**.

BAC is the universal standard for measuring alcohol impairment. The legal limit for driving in all US states is 0.08% BAC. Impairment begins at much lower levels — even 0.02–0.03% BAC affects judgment, reaction time, and divided attention. The relationship between drinks consumed and BAC varies significantly by body weight, sex, food intake, and individual metabolism.

## BAC Levels and Effects

| BAC Level | Legal Status | Typical Effects |
|----------|-------------|----------------|
| 0.01–0.03% | Below legal limit | Relaxation; slight impairment in divided attention |
| 0.04–0.06% | Below legal limit | Warmth, relaxation; clearly impaired judgment |
| 0.07–0.09% | Near/at legal limit | Significant impairment; 0.08% is legal DUI threshold |
| 0.10–0.12% | Illegal to drive | Clear impairment of balance, speech, reaction time |
| 0.13–0.15% | Illegal | Gross impairment; alcohol-related nausea begins |
| 0.16–0.20% | Illegal | Disorientation; possible blackout threshold |
| 0.20–0.30% | Dangerously high | Confusion, stupor, loss of consciousness possible |
| 0.30%+ | Potentially fatal | Coma risk; respiratory depression |

## Factors That Affect BAC

**Body weight:** Heavier individuals have more body mass to dilute alcohol. A 250 lb person reaches lower BAC from the same drinks as a 120 lb person.

**Biological sex:** Women have a lower percentage of body water (55% vs. 68% for men on average), meaning alcohol is more concentrated in their bloodstream. Women also have lower levels of alcohol dehydrogenase (the enzyme that metabolizes alcohol in the stomach). The same number of drinks produces higher BAC in women than men of equal weight.

**Food intake:** Food in the stomach slows alcohol absorption. Drinking on an empty stomach can increase peak BAC by 50% vs. drinking with a meal.

**Drink strength and type:** Standard drinks each contain approximately 14 grams of pure alcohol: 12 oz beer (5% ABV), 5 oz wine (12% ABV), or 1.5 oz spirits (40% ABV). Craft beers, cocktails, and wine vary significantly in alcohol content.

## Alcohol Metabolism Rate

The body metabolizes alcohol at approximately 0.015% BAC per hour (on average). This is roughly equivalent to one standard drink per hour. Time to reach 0.00% BAC:

| Starting BAC | Hours to Sober Up |
|-------------|------------------|
| 0.05% | ~3.3 hours |
| 0.08% | ~5.3 hours |
| 0.10% | ~6.7 hours |
| 0.15% | ~10 hours |
| 0.20% | ~13.3 hours |

**Coffee, water, food, exercise, and cold showers do NOT speed up this process.** Metabolism rate is biological — it cannot be accelerated. Time is the only way to reduce BAC.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many drinks does it take to reach 0.08% BAC?**
There is no single answer — it depends entirely on body weight, sex, time elapsed, and food intake. General approximation for a 150 lb male, no food, within 2 hours: approximately 4–5 standard drinks reaches approximately 0.08%. For a 120 lb female: approximately 3 drinks may reach 0.08%. These are approximations only — individual variation is substantial. The safe answer to "can I drive?" is never to estimate based on number of drinks. Use designated drivers, rideshares, or public transit.

**Does the type of alcohol matter (beer vs. wine vs. spirits)?**
The type of beverage does not affect how alcohol is processed — only the total amount of ethanol consumed matters. A 12 oz beer, 5 oz glass of wine, and 1.5 oz shot of spirits each contain approximately 14 grams of pure ethanol. "I only drank beer" or "it was just wine" does not change BAC — the amount of alcohol is what matters. Carbonation (champagne, beer) may slightly speed initial absorption; otherwise the source beverage is irrelevant.

**Is it possible to have 0.00% BAC and still be impaired?**
Yes — fatigue, prescription medications, cannabis, and other drugs can impair driving even with 0.00% BAC. Drug-impaired driving (DUID — Driving Under the Influence of Drugs) is illegal and increasingly enforced. Cannabis significantly impairs driving; opioids and benzodiazepines (anti-anxiety medications) dramatically impair reaction time and coordination. Unlike alcohol, there is no universally accepted per-se legal limit for cannabis or other drugs in most states.

**How accurate are breathalyzers?**
Professional law enforcement breathalyzers (Intoxilyzer, DataMaster) are generally accurate within ±5% when properly maintained and calibrated. Consumer-grade breathalyzers can have errors of 15–20% or more. Factors that can cause false positives: certain medical conditions (diabetes or low-carb diets producing acetone), certain foods, mouth alcohol from recent drinking. Law enforcement protocols include waiting periods (typically 15–20 minutes without alcohol or belching) to minimize mouth alcohol contamination.

**How long does alcohol stay in your system for a drug test?**
Breathalyzers detect alcohol for up to 12–24 hours. Blood tests: 12–24 hours after drinking. Urine: standard urine tests detect alcohol for 12–24 hours; EtG (ethyl glucuronide) urine tests can detect recent alcohol consumption for 72–80 hours after last drink, and some tests detect up to 120 hours. Hair follicle tests can detect alcohol use over the past 90 days through EtG and EtS markers, though these are less common for alcohol than for drugs.
