Ovulation typically occurs approximately 14 days before your next period — not necessarily 14 days after your last period. For a 28-day cycle, ovulation is around Day 14. For a 35-day cycle, ovulation is around Day 21. For a 24-day cycle, ovulation is around Day 10. The fertile window spans the 5 days before ovulation and the day of ovulation — 6 days total — because sperm can survive in the reproductive tract for up to 5 days.

If you are trying to conceive (TTC), timing intercourse to the fertile window significantly increases the monthly probability of conception. The probability of conception from unprotected intercourse is approximately 25–30% in the most fertile 2-day window and drops substantially outside it. Understanding when you ovulate is the single most actionable change you can make when trying to get pregnant.

## How to Use the Ovulation Calculator

1. **Enter the first day of your last period.** This is Day 1 of your current cycle.
2. **Enter your average cycle length.** Count from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. If your cycle varies, use the average of the last 3–6 cycles. Normal cycle length: 21–35 days.
3. **Enter your luteal phase length (optional).** The luteal phase is the time from ovulation to your next period. It is typically 12–16 days and is more consistent than the follicular phase. If unknown, the calculator defaults to 14 days.
4. **Read the results.** Estimated ovulation date, 6-day fertile window, next expected period, and peak fertility days (the 2 days with highest conception probability).

## The Ovulation Formula

**Estimated ovulation day:**
```
Ovulation Day = Cycle Length − Luteal Phase Length
```

**Fertile window:**
```
Fertile Window Start = Ovulation Day − 5
Fertile Window End   = Ovulation Day
Peak Fertility       = Ovulation Day − 1 and Ovulation Day
```

**Worked example — 32-day cycle:**

```
Luteal phase (typical): 14 days
Ovulation Day: 32 − 14 = Day 18

If last period started January 1:
Ovulation estimate: January 18
Fertile window: January 13–18
Peak fertility: January 17–18
Next expected period: February 2
```

## Signs of Ovulation to Track

Calculator estimates assume regular cycles. Physical signs provide real-time confirmation:

**Cervical Mucus Changes:**
The most reliable daily indicator. As ovulation approaches:
- Days after period: Dry (little to no discharge)
- Pre-ovulation: Sticky, white/cream discharge
- Approaching ovulation: Watery discharge
- At ovulation: Egg white cervical mucus (EWCM) — clear, slippery, stretchy like raw egg whites. This indicates peak fertility.
- After ovulation: Returns to sticky/creamy then dry

**Basal Body Temperature (BBT):**
Resting temperature rises 0.2–0.5°F (0.1–0.3°C) after ovulation due to progesterone. Track BBT every morning before getting out of bed (same time each day). The temperature rise confirms ovulation has occurred. The limitation: temperature rises after ovulation, so it is more useful for identifying patterns over months than for timing current-cycle intercourse.

**Ovulation Predictor Kits (OPKs):**
Urine strips that detect the LH surge that occurs 24–36 hours before ovulation. The LH surge is a reliable predictor of imminent ovulation. Positive OPK = ovulation likely within 12–36 hours — time to be sexually active in the next 1–2 days. OPKs provide more real-time accuracy than calendar-based calculators.

**Mittelschmerz:**
One-sided pelvic pain or cramping around ovulation, experienced by some women. Can be useful confirmation when combined with other signs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How accurate are ovulation calculators?**
Ovulation calculators are estimates based on average cycle length and a standard luteal phase. They are most accurate for women with regular, predictable cycles. Irregular cycles (PCOS, perimenopause, stress, recent hormonal contraceptive discontinuation) make calendar-based prediction unreliable. For more accuracy, combine the calculator with BBT tracking and OPKs. Research suggests ovulation occurs within 2 days of the calculator's estimate in most women with regular cycles.

**Can you get pregnant outside the fertile window?**
While the fertile window (5 days before + day of ovulation) encompasses all realistic conception opportunities, ovulation timing can vary even in regular cycles — as much as 1–3 days earlier or later than expected. This is why abstinence-based birth control using the calendar method has a 14–24% typical-use failure rate per year. The fertile window is a probability guide, not an absolute boundary.

**Does ovulation always happen on the same day each cycle?**
No — even in women with very regular cycles, ovulation can shift by 1–5 days due to stress, illness, travel, sleep disruption, or exercise changes. The luteal phase is more consistent (typically 12–16 days) than the follicular phase (which varies). This is why BBT charting and OPKs provide more reliable real-time ovulation detection than purely calendar-based prediction.

**How long after ovulation can an egg be fertilized?**
The egg is only viable for approximately 12–24 hours after release. This is why the most fertile days are the 2 days before ovulation (when sperm is already present when the egg is released) rather than the day after ovulation (when the egg may already be no longer viable). The common advice to "have sex every other day during the fertile window" optimizes sperm availability around ovulation rather than chasing ovulation after the fact.

**Can lifestyle factors affect ovulation?**
Yes. Factors that can disrupt or delay ovulation: severe caloric restriction or underweight (can stop ovulation entirely — hypothalamic amenorrhea), excessive exercise, extreme stress, thyroid disorders, PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome), significant weight changes, and certain medications. Regular ovulation requires hormonal balance — addressing underlying health factors is important if cycles are very irregular (more than 7 days variation in cycle length).
