Reading speed is measured in words per minute (WPM). The average adult reads approximately 200–250 WPM for non-fiction text with good comprehension. Speed readers can reach 400–700 WPM with training. To calculate how long it will take to read a document, divide the total word count by your reading speed: a 5,000-word article read at 250 WPM takes 5,000 ÷ 250 = 20 minutes.

Knowing your reading speed helps you plan time accurately for reading assignments, business documents, books, and research papers. The reading time estimates shown on articles (like "8 min read" at the top of this article) use 200–250 WPM as the baseline — the actual time varies by reading speed, material complexity, and familiarity with the topic.

**Key insight:** Comprehension typically decreases as reading speed increases beyond 400–500 WPM for most people. Speed reading claims of 1,000–5,000 WPM with full comprehension are largely not supported by cognitive science research — at these speeds, the brain is typically skimming for keywords rather than reading and comprehending.

## How to Increase Reading Speed

**Evidence-based strategies:**

**1. Eliminate subvocalization (silent speech).** Most readers mentally "hear" each word as they read. Reducing this inner voice allows faster reading. Practice: read while humming or mouthing "one-two-three" to occupy the speech mechanism.

**2. Use a pointer or guide.** Moving a finger or pen across the page guides the eye and prevents backtracking (re-reading). This alone often increases speed 20–30%.

**3. Expand your focus span.** Train yourself to read chunks of 3–4 words at once rather than one word at a time. Practice by visually fixating on the center of each phrase.

**4. Minimize regression.** Most readers subconsciously re-read 15–25% of text. Making a conscious effort to read forward without regressing can increase speed significantly.

**5. Read more, broadly.** The more familiar you are with vocabulary and the subject matter, the faster you process text. Wide reading builds the vocabulary and background knowledge that enables faster processing.

**What does not work:** Eveline/RSVP speed reading software (flashing one word at a time at 500+ WPM) improves raw word exposure speed but severely impairs comprehension — the brain needs time to build mental models from text, not just decode individual words.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does audiobook listening count as "reading"?**
For comprehension and learning purposes, audiobooks and reading produce largely equivalent outcomes — both are effective for absorbing content. Average audiobook listening speed is 150–160 WPM (natural speech rate); many listeners speed up to 1.25–2× speed (200–300 WPM equivalent). For technical or complex material, reading may allow better comprehension because you can easily re-read confusing passages. For narrative or motivational content, audiobooks are equally effective and allow multitasking.

**How is reading speed measured with comprehension?**
True reading speed assessments include a comprehension test after reading. The "adjusted WPM" multiplies raw WPM by comprehension accuracy: if you read at 350 WPM but only retain 70% of content, your effective reading speed is 350 × 0.70 = 245 WPM. A person reading at 250 WPM with 95% comprehension is often more effective than a speed reader at 600 WPM with 50% comprehension.

**Does reading on screens affect speed vs. reading on paper?**
Most research shows people read slightly slower on screens (10–30%) with slightly reduced comprehension for longer texts. However, this gap has narrowed as reading on screens has become more familiar. E-readers (e-ink displays) perform comparably to paper; backlit LCD screens may cause more eye fatigue. For extended reading, adjusting screen brightness, using night mode, and increasing font size reduces fatigue and can improve reading efficiency on screens.

**What is the fastest anyone has ever read?**
Speed reading claims are disputed. Howard Stephen Berg is cited in the Guinness Book of Records at 25,000 WPM — but independent verification of comprehension at this rate does not exist. Kim Peek (the inspiration for Rain Man) reportedly read two pages simultaneously, one with each eye. World speed reading champions typically compete at 1,000–4,000 WPM with comprehension tests — scores that are real but represent a tiny fraction of people after years of training.

**How do I estimate the word count of a printed book?**
Count words in a sample of 10 typical text lines, divide by 10 for average words per line, then multiply by lines per page × pages. Or use the average: most paperback novels have 250–300 words per page. So a 350-page novel has approximately 87,500–105,000 words.
